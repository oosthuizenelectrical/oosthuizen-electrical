How much does solar cost in Cape Town?

Typical 2026 price ranges for solar and battery systems, what pushes the price up or down, and how to compare quotes properly.

"How much will solar cost me?" is the first question almost everyone asks. The honest answer is that it depends on what you want the system to do. Here are typical 2026 price ranges in South Africa, what drives them, and how to compare quotes.

Typical price ranges in 2026

Published South African price guides for 2026 put fully installed systems roughly in these ranges:

  • Inverter and battery backup only (no panels): the cheapest way to keep the lights, Wi-Fi and TV on during outages. It doesn't lower your electricity bill, because the battery charges from the grid.
  • About 5kW of solar panels with a hybrid inverter, no battery: roughly R75 000 to R100 000.
  • About 5kW of solar with a hybrid inverter and a 10kWh lithium battery: roughly R90 000 to R165 000, depending on brands and the job.
  • Larger homes (8kW and up): from around R180 000.

Treat these as a ballpark only. Your price depends on your home, your usage and the equipment you choose. We don't publish fixed prices, because a quote that ignores your roof and distribution board (DB) isn't worth much. Our quotes are free.

What pushes the price up or down

  • Battery size. The battery is usually the biggest single cost. More storage means longer backup and more of your solar used at night.
  • Inverter brand and size. Brands like Sunsynk, Deye, Victron and Growatt sit at different price points. Bigger inverters run more appliances at once.
  • Number of panels. More panels means more daytime generation and bigger savings on your bill.
  • Your roof. Tile, IBR or flat roofs, height, and how far the panels are from the DB all affect labour and materials.
  • Your DB. Older boards sometimes need upgrading so the system can be wired in safely and split into essential and non-essential circuits.
  • Paperwork. Grid-tied systems in Cape Town must be registered with the City (SSEG), which includes an engineer's sign-off. Make sure it is in the quote. See our SSEG registration guide.

How to compare quotes properly

  • Check that each quote lists the exact inverter, battery (in kWh) and panel models and quantities.
  • Ask whether the COC, SSEG registration and engineer sign-off are included.
  • Ask what workmanship warranty you get, on top of the manufacturer warranties. Ours is 1 year.
  • Make sure the installer is a registered electrical contractor. Ours is WC 04004.
  • Be wary of quotes far cheaper than the rest. The difference usually shows up in the equipment or the wiring.

Will it pay for itself?

Systems with panels lower your electricity bill, and many homeowners see payback within several years, depending on usage, tariffs and system size. A backup-only system is insurance against outages rather than a saving. We'll talk you through which makes sense for your home.
